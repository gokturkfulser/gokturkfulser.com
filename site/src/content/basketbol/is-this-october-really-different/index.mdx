Jalen Williams’s left leg bent under him at an angle a leg should never bend, and the season wasn’t even a week old. The next day came the news that Anthony Edwards would need knee surgery. Then Banchero, Haliburton, Duren, Kawhi. Within a few days I was reading the injury report more closely than the rosters.

In the visitors’ locker room in Milwaukee, someone asked Kevin Durant why this was happening. He said he didn’t know, and that he wished he did. Ime Udoka was less careful: he said he had never seen a picture like this so early in a season.

I couldn’t shake the thought that we say the same sentence every October. Last year it was “injuries are way up,” and the year before that too. So I’m not going to hunt for a cure here. All I want to know is whether the numbers we have really separate the start of this season from the ones before it.

## Thunder’s bad week, and everyone else’s

Oklahoma City sums up the list best. A team with one of the deepest rosters in the league lost Jalen Williams (broken fibula, dislocated ankle), Cason Wallace (torn labrum in the shoulder, surgery) and Chet Holmgren for a long stretch, all in a single week. For the first two, most of the season is gone, maybe all of it.

It isn’t pretty elsewhere either. Edwards is out two months. Ja Morant strained his hamstring (grade 2) in his first game against his old team, Memphis, and will miss at least four weeks. Kawhi’s knee swelled up again, and three to four weeks is the talk. Orlando lost two players on one play: Suggs landed on Banchero’s ankle, so Banchero is out four to six weeks and Suggs, who has a bone bruise in his knee, at least two. Haliburton strained his hamstring in his fourth game back after a full season of Achilles rehab, and he’s out at least two weeks. Jaren Jackson Jr. has an MCL injury, six to eight weeks, and Duren’s hamstring will keep him out at least two.

And that’s only what happened after the season started. Ben Simmons, in the final act of a comeback story that has been told for years, tore his Achilles in practice days before opening night. Ingram and Beal weren’t ready for the season, and Kon Knueppel still hasn’t played.

On October 28, the fifth game day of the season, the official injury report carried 103 names. Eighty of them were there because of injury or illness.

## So what do the numbers say?

The person best placed to answer that is Jeff Stotts. He’s a certified athletic trainer who has been logging NBA injuries in his own database since 2005, and every week he totals the games the league misses to injury or illness. He keeps rest days separate, which matters in this debate.

According to Stotts, 176 games were lost in the first week of 2026-27, meaning the six days from October 20 to 25. Thanks to the numbers Marc Stein pulled, again from Stotts, we can compare that directly with the first six days of the previous two seasons:

| Season | Period | Games missed | Games played | Lost per game |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-27 | First 6 days | 176 | 43 | 4.1 |
| 2024-25 | First 6 days | 151 | 41 | 3.7 |
| 2023-24 | First 6 days | 118 | 40 | 2.9 |

This season has had two or three more games played, but that isn’t enough to explain the gap. It works out to about 4.1 games lost for every game played: 11% more than the same stretch of 2024-25 and 39% more than 2023-24. It’s only a three-season comparison, but the direction is clear.

Widening the window, the same direction shows up. Games lost in the first eight weeks were 1,579 in 2023-24, 1,962 in 2024-25 and 2,062 in 2025-26. Every opening has been worse than the one before for three seasons running, and the first week of 2026-27 isn’t stepping down that staircase.

## Zooming out to twenty years

Look at the seasons one by one and you see ups and downs; zoom out to twenty years and you see a slope. In 2024-25, 6,779 games were missed to injury and illness. The only other season over 6,000 in Stotts’s records is 2021-22, when Omicron emptied the locker rooms. 2025-26 closed week 23 at 6,177. Between 2006 and 2017 the season total topped 5,000 only once; it has done so in seven of the last eight seasons.

Dr. Brian Sutterer, a physician known for analyzing basketball injuries, looked at what that slope is made of. In 2005-06, players missed 10.8% of games to musculoskeletal problems; in 2025-26 that figure is 21.2%. A player who logs more than 20 minutes a game used to miss 7.5 games a season on average; now it’s 15.4.

<GrafikSakatlikOranlari dil="en" />

The most interesting part for me is that injuries are getting shorter. Twenty years ago a rotation player was hurt 0.9 times a season and each time he was gone for 8 games. Today it’s 3.2 times, and each stint is 4.4 games. A league that breaks more often and recovers faster. The serious injuries aren’t going away, either: the rate of those lasting more than 20 games is up about 70% from 2005-2012 to 2019-2026. ACL and Achilles tears are up too, though Sutterer himself says it’s hard to draw a trend from a few cases a year.

## The league has its own math

The NBA pushes back on these numbers, and it isn’t entirely wrong to. Last December Adam Silver said the league’s data showed the lowest injury count in three years. The league also announced that injuries that cost stars games fell 25% in a year. In those same weeks, Stotts’s total was going up.

Both can be true at once. The league counts injury events and defines a “star” as a player who has made All-Star or All-NBA in the last three seasons. Stotts adds up games lost across every roster. A small number of long injuries makes the league’s figure look low and Stotts’s look high. Even for stars, there’s no need to exaggerate: in 2023-24 and 2024-25 they missed 19% of games, the same as in 2017-19 and well below the 27% of 2019-20.

The measurement itself has changed, too. Medical staffs sit a player down over even a minor tweak, and the official report is more detailed than it used to be. Sutterer admits part of the rise could come from that caution and from differences in record-keeping, and adds that he can’t measure how much.

There’s also a timing problem. Jalen Williams’s injury shows up in the table today as a few games; by March it will be sixty or seventy. October’s bill gets cut in November and December.

## My read

Is it on the same level? No. This season opened on top of a line that was already rising. But I don’t see a blow-up that breaks from the past, either. Every October since 2023 has been worse than the one before, and this is the latest link in that chain.

What feels different is less the number than who’s being counted. Three or four All-Stars gone for months in a single week, a title contender losing three players at once: that speaks a lot louder than a reasonable bump in the total.

I’ll wait for the end of November. If Stotts’s eight-week total comfortably clears 2,062, Udoka will have been right.
